# Dialect Master - Content Design

## Pedagogical Approach

### Core Philosophy
1. **Heritage-first**: Meet users where they are — they already understand more than they realize
2. **Tone-early**: Introduce tones from Day 1 with heavy practice
3. **Context-rich**: Every phrase taught in a real scenario, not isolated vocabulary
4. **Production-driven**: Focus on speaking, not just recognition
5. **Cultural-embedded**: Language learning is cultural learning

### Learning Science Principles
- **Spaced repetition**: Review intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days
- **Active recall**: Testing beats re-reading
- **Chunking**: Teach phrases, not just words
- **Dual-coding**: Pair audio with visual (tone ladders, characters)
- **Interleaving**: Mix vocabulary, pronunciation, and conversation practice

---

## Lesson Structure

### Micro-Learning Format
- **Duration**: 5-7 minutes per lesson
- **Commitment**: 1-2 lessons per day = habit formation
- **Flexible**: Can stop mid-lesson and resume later

### Lesson Anatomy (7 Steps)

```
1. WARM-UP (45 sec)
   └── Review previous lesson's key phrases
   └── Streak check-in

2. NEW VOCABULARY (90 sec)
   └── 5-7 new words/phrases with audio
   └── Visual: character + romanization + meaning
   └── Slow playback option

3. PRONUNCIATION LAB (60 sec)
   └── Focus on 1-2 tricky sounds/tones
   └── Interactive: hear → repeat → compare
   └── Minimal pair drills

4. DIALOGUE EXPOSURE (90 sec)
   └── Listen to native speaker dialogue
   └── Read along with transcript
   └── Key phrase highlight

5. COMPREHENSION CHECK (45 sec)
   └── 2-3 quick questions
   └── Audio-based (not text-based)
   └── Immediate feedback

6. PRODUCTION PRACTICE (60 sec)
   └── Record yourself saying phrase
   └── Compare to native audio
   └── Score based on tone + pronunciation

7. CULTURAL NOTE (30 sec)
   └── One cultural insight
   └── Singapore context
   └── Motivation or story
```

## Tone Training System

### Tone Introduction Order

**Phase 1: Tone 1 (High Level) - Lesson 1**
- Easy to hear and produce
- Consistent vowel length
- Examples: 你 (lí), 三 (sam), 公 (kong)

**Phase 2: Tone 7 (Entering) - Lesson 2**
- Short, clipped ending
- Stop consonant
- Examples: 六 (li̍k), 七 (chhit), 食 (chia̤h)

**Phase 3: Tone 3 (High Falling) - Lesson 3**
- Long descent
- Examples: 四 (sì), 爸 (pē), 去 (khì)

**Phase 4: Tone 2 (Rising) - Lesson 4**
- Rising contour
- Examples: 二 (jī), 疼 (thiàⁿ)

**Phase 5: Tone 5 (Low Level) - Lesson 5**
- Low, steady
- Examples: 五 (gō̤), 眠 (bîn)

**Phase 6: Tone 6 (Low Rising) - Lesson 6**
- Rising from low
- Examples: 耳 (hīⁿ), 牛 (gû)

### Tone Drill Types

**Type 1: Minimal Pairs**
| Tone 1 | Tone 3 | Tone 6 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| 诗 (si) poetry | 死 (sí) die | 时 (sî) time |
| 夫 (hu) husband | 苦 (khóu) bitter | 糊 (hôo) glue |
| 孤 (ko) lonely | 古 (kóu) old | 五 (gō̤) five |

**Type 2: Tone Ladders**
Visual ladder showing each tone's contour:
```
Tone 1: ───────── (high, flat)
Tone 2:     ╱      (rising)
Tone 3:  ╲          (falling)
Tone 5: _ _ _ _ _    (low, flat)
Tone 6:    ╲_        (low rising)
Tone 7: ─┐           (short, stopped)
```

**Type 3: Tone Identification**
- Hear tone → tap corresponding button
- Progressively faster

**Type 4: Production Practice**
- See tone name → say word
- Visual feedback showing pitch contour
- Compare to native speaker

## Scenario Design Framework

### Scenario Elements

1. **Setting** - Where does conversation happen?
2. **Characters** - Who is speaking? (age, relationship, formality)
3. **Goal** - What does speaker want?
4. **Key Phrases** - 5-7 essential phrases
5. **Cultural Notes** - Context, etiquette, variations
6. **Practice Prompts** - Simulated situations

### Sample Scenario: Hawker Center

**Setting**: Char Kway Teow stall, 11:30 AM
**Characters**: Customer (young adult), Uncle/Auntie (hawker)
**Goal**: Order food, specify preferences

**Key Phrases**:
1. "Uncle, 一盘炒粿条" (Uncle, one plate of char kway teow)
2. "不要辣" (No spicy)
3. "要大要小?" (Large or small?)
4. "在中吃还是打包?" (Eat here or takeaway?)
5. "总共三块半" (Total $3.50)

**Cultural Notes**:
- "Uncle/Auntie" is respectful for older hawkers
- Pointing at what you want is acceptable
- Saying "不要辣" even if you want mild spice

**Practice Prompts**:
1. You want it spicy. What do you say?
2. You're vegetarian. What can you order?
3. You want takeaway for 2 people.

## Cultural Integration

### Every Lesson Includes

1. **Cultural Hook** (30 sec at start)
   - Story, proverb, or cultural fact
   - Relates to lesson topic

2. **Cultural Note** (30 sec at end)
   - Usage tips
   - Do's and don'ts
   - Singapore context

### Sample Cultural Notes

**For Food Unit:**
> "In Singapore, asking '食饱未?' (Have you eaten?) is not just about food — it's a way of showing care. Your auntie might force-feed you even if you say yes!"

**For Family Unit:**
> "In traditional Hokkien families, older relatives are addressed with specific terms. Calling your grandma '阿嬷' (a-má) instead of just '妈' shows respect and differentiates from your mom."

**For Numbers Unit:**
> "When bargaining at markets, Singaporeans often say '算汝啦!' (For you!) — even if it's already a good price. It's part of the fun, not a real negotiation."

## Content Quality Standards

### Audio Requirements
- **Native speakers**: Record with Singaporean Hokkien speakers
- **Multiple voices**: Include male, female, young, old
- **Natural pace**: Normal speed + 0.75x slow version
- **Clear enunciation**: For beginners
- **Ambient options**: Market, hawker center, home

### Visual Requirements
- **Tone contours**: Animated pitch visualization
- **Romanization**: Large, clear, color-coded tones
- **Characters**: Traditional + simplified where applicable
- **Cultural images**: Real Singapore photos

### Accessibility
- **Offline mode**: All audio downloadable
- **Dark mode**: Reduce eye strain
- **Large text option**: For visibility
- **Captions**: All audio has text
